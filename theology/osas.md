   OSAS often includes the idea of **eternal rewards**. A believer who continues in sin might still be saved, but “as through fire” (1 Cor 3:15), and will miss out on rewards, joy, and glory.

However, critics of OSAS would say that this still minimizes the seriousness of sin, and creates a loophole for spiritual negligence.

This was not an isolated teaching. It echoes throughout the New Testament:

---

### **Jesus’ Teaching**
- **Mark 1:15** – “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; *repent and believe in the gospel*.”
- **Luke 13:3** – “*Unless you repent, you will all likewise perish.*”

---

### **Apostolic Teaching**
- **Acts 3:19** – “*Repent, then, and turn to God, so that your sins may be wiped out*…”
- **Acts 17:30** – “God now commands *all people everywhere to repent*.”
- **2 Corinthians 7:10** – “*Godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation…*”

---

### **What is Repentance?**
Repentance is not just feeling sorry or guilty. Biblically, it includes:
- A change of **mind** about sin and God.
- A turning away from sin.
- A turning toward God and His will.

---

### **How This Challenges OSAS**
If someone says:
> “I was saved years ago, but I never repented, and I don’t want to stop sinning…”

The New Testament would challenge whether that person was ever genuinely converted. A heart that has truly received the grace of God is a *repentant* heart—a heart in process, one that grieves sin, turns from it, and walks toward God, even if imperfectly.

So yes—Peter’s command in Acts 2 isn’t an optional step. It is foundational to entering into salvation.
