# 🧠 Deep Dive into the **Attic Greek Genitive Case**

---

## 🏛️ I. Core Identity of the Genitive

### 🔤 *Form*:
- The genitive case typically answers the question **“of whom?” or “of what?”**
- It appears across **all three declensions**, with masculine, feminine, and neuter genders.

---

## 🧩 II. Primary Functions (Syntax + Semantics)

We categorize genitive usage into **syntactic (structural)** and **semantic (meaning-based)** categories.

---

### 🧱 A. **Syntactic Genitive (Structural Roles)**

#### 1. 📌 **Adnominal Genitive** (“of”-genitive)
- Modifies a noun, often expressing **possession** or **association**.
- Example:
  > ἡ τοῦ ἀνδρὸς οἰκία — *the house of the man*
- Subtypes:
  - **Possessive Genitive**: literal or metaphorical possession.
  - **Genitive of Characteristic**: describes quality.
    - Example: ἀνὴρ σοφίας — *a man of wisdom*
  - **Material Genitive**: indicates what something is made of.
    - Example: δακτύλιος χρυσίου — *a ring of gold*

#### 2. 📌 **Partitive Genitive**
- Indicates the **whole from which a part is taken**.
- Often used with:
  - pronouns (τις, ἕκαστος)
  - numbers (δύο τῶν στρατιωτῶν — *two of the soldiers*)
  - superlatives (ὁ σοφώτατος τῶν ἀνθρώπων — *the wisest of men*)

#### 3. 📌 **Genitive with Verbs**
- Verbs that **imply separation, touching, ruling, remembering, etc.** often take genitive objects.
- Subcategories:
  - **Verbs of Separation/Removal**: ἀφαιρέομαι, ἀπαλλάττω
  - **Verbs of Perception**: ἀκούω, γεύομαι
  - **Verbs of Emotion**: ἐπιθυμέω, ἐρῶ
  - **Verbs of Rule/Mastery**: ἄρχω, κρατέω

#### 4. 📌 **Genitive with Prepositions**
- Many prepositions govern the genitive, especially those indicating:
  - **separation** (ἀπό)
  - **origin/source** (ἐκ, ἀπό)
  - **association** (μετά, σύν)
  - **place** (ὑπέρ, ὑπό)
- Example: ἀπὸ τῆς οἰκίας — *from the house*

---

### 🎯 B. **Semantic Genitive (Meaning-Based Roles)**

#### 1. 💠 **Possessive**
- Indicates ownership.
- Example: τοῦ ἀνδρὸς παῖς — *the man's child*

#### 2. 💠 **Objective vs. Subjective Genitive**
- **Subjective**: genitive noun is the **subject** of the verbal idea in the noun.
  - ὁ φόβος τοῦ θεοῦ — *the fear that the god feels*
- **Objective**: genitive noun is the **object** of the verbal idea in the noun.
  - ὁ φόβος τοῦ θεοῦ — *the fear of the god (felt by someone else)*
- Determined contextually. Some nouns allow both readings.

#### 3. 💠 **Descriptive Genitive**
- Describes a characteristic/quality.
- Often poetic or elevated style.
- Example: γυνὴ ἀνδρὸς ἀρίστου — *a woman of a noble man* (i.e., noble herself)

#### 4. 💠 **Genitive of Cause/Reason**
- Indicates why something happens.
- Example: χαίρω τῆς νίκης — *I rejoice because of the victory*

#### 5. 💠 **Genitive of Price/Value**
- Expresses cost or worth.
- Example: ὠνέομαι ἑκατὸν δραχμῶν — *I buy for 100 drachmas*

#### 6. 💠 **Genitive of Measure/Extent**
- With time, distance, or degree.
- Example: μεῖνον ὀλίγου χρόνου — *stay for a short time*
- Example: ἑνὸς σταδίου — *a stadium in length*

#### 7. 💠 **Genitive of Comparison**
- Used with comparative adjectives or adverbs.
- Example: σοφώτερος τοῦ Σωκράτους — *wiser than Socrates*

---

### 8. 🕰️ Genitive of Time Within Which

#### 🏷️ Syntactic Category:
- This is a **genitive of measure**, but more specifically, it refers to **time “within which” something happens** — often contrasted with the **dative of time “when”** and **accusative of time “duration”**.

---

#### 📌 Definition:
The **Genitive of Time Within Which** indicates the time *during the course of which* or *within which* something occurs.

- It answers the question: **"within what time frame?"**
- Most commonly seen with **temporal nouns** in the **genitive singular or plural**.

---

#### 🔠 Example:
**Greek:** τούτων τῶν ἡμερῶν ἥκω.  
**Translation:** *I have arrived during these days* / *within these days*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (τούτων τῶν ἡμερῶν) sets a time frame in which the action of arrival takes place.

---

#### ⚖️ Compare:
| Construction | Case       | Example                         | Translation                        |
|--------------|------------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Time **when**      | Dative     | τῇ τρίτῃ ἡμέρᾳ                  | *on the third day*                 |
| Time **within which** | Genitive   | τριῶν ἡμερῶν                     | *within three days*                |
| Time **how long**      | Accusative | τρεῖς ἡμέρας                     | *for three days*                   |

---

#### 🧠 More Examples:

1. **Greek:** τοῦ θέρους ἔπλεον εἰς Ἀσίαν  
   **Translation:** *They sailed to Asia during the summer*  
   **Genitive of Time Within Which** — τοῦ θέρους

2. **Greek:** χειμῶνος οὐκ ἐμάχομεθα  
   **Translation:** *We did not fight during the winter*  
   **Genitive of Time Within Which** — χειμῶνος

---

#### 🧩 Summary:
- This construction is **temporal** in nature.
- It's **common in historiography** (e.g. Herodotus, Thucydides).
- It sets the **period or range** within which an event occurs.

---

## 🌀 III. Complex & Rare Uses

#### 1. 🔍 **Genitive Absolute**
- A participial phrase **detached** from the main sentence syntactically.
- Subject + Participle both in genitive.
- Example: τοῦ ἀνδρὸς ἐλθόντος, ἐχάρησαν. — *When the man came, they rejoiced.*
- Usually sets temporal, causal, or concessive context.

#### 2. 🧵 **Genitive of Respect (Specification)**
- Limits or qualifies an assertion.
- Example: σοφὸς τῇ ἀριθμητικῇ — *wise in arithmetic*

#### 3. 💬 **Genitive with Impersonal Expressions**
- ἐξόν ἐστι + gen., δέον ἐστι + gen. = *it is permitted/necessary for someone*.
- Example: δέον τοῦ στρατηγοῦ λέγειν — *it being necessary for the general to speak*

#### 4. 🧬 **Genitive in Poetry**
- Much freer, includes:
  - transferred genitives
  - extended partitive uses
  - genitive in apposition to phrases

---

## 🛠️ IV. Genitive with Special Constructions

### 1. **Double Genitive**
- Common with abstract nouns.
- Example: ἡ ἐπιμέλεια τῶν παίδων τοῦ πατρός — *the care of the children by the father*

### 2. **Genitive with Infinitives**
- Especially with impersonal expressions or expressions of need.
- Example: δεῖ σε μαθήσεως — *you have need of learning*

- 🧩 **Explanation**

---

### 1. 🏷️ Possessive Genitive (Adnominal)
**Greek:** ἡ τοῦ ἀνδρὸς οἰκία  
**Translation:** *The house of the man*  
**Explanation:** The genitive noun (τοῦ ἀνδρὸς) expresses possession of the house.

---

### 2. 🏷️ Genitive of Characteristic
**Greek:** ἀνὴρ σοφίας  
**Translation:** *A man of wisdom*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (σοφίας) describes a characteristic or quality possessed by the noun.

---

### 3. 🏷️ Material Genitive
**Greek:** δακτύλιος χρυσίου  
**Translation:** *A ring of gold*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (χρυσίου) indicates the material out of which the ring is made.

---

### 4. 🏷️ Partitive Genitive
**Greek:** εἷς τῶν στρατιωτῶν  
**Translation:** *One of the soldiers*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (τῶν στρατιωτῶν) represents the whole group from which one is taken.

---

### 5. 🏷️ Genitive with Verb of Perception
**Greek:** ἀκούω τοῦ παιδός  
**Translation:** *I hear the boy*  
**Explanation:** Certain perception verbs like ἀκούω take their object in the genitive.

---

### 6. 🏷️ Genitive with Verb of Emotion
**Greek:** ἐπιθυμεῖ τῆς σοφίας  
**Translation:** *He desires wisdom*  
**Explanation:** Some verbs of desire and longing govern the genitive case.

---

### 7. 🏷️ Genitive with Preposition (Origin)
**Greek:** ἐκ τῆς οἰκίας ἐξῆλθεν  
**Translation:** *He went out of the house*  
**Explanation:** Preposition ἐκ (*out of*) governs the genitive, indicating source/origin.

---

### 8. 🏷️ Subjective Genitive
**Greek:** ὁ φόβος τοῦ πολέμου  
**Translation:** *The fear caused by the war*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (τοῦ πολέμου) acts as the subject of the fear.

---

### 9. 🏷️ Objective Genitive
**Greek:** ὁ φόβος τοῦ θεοῦ  
**Translation:** *Fear of the god*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (τοῦ θεοῦ) is the object of the fear—what is feared.

---

### 10. 🏷️ Genitive of Cause
**Greek:** χαίρω τῆς νίκης  
**Translation:** *I rejoice because of the victory*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (τῆς νίκης) expresses the cause or reason for the emotion.

---

### 11. 🏷️ Genitive of Price
**Greek:** ὠνέομαι πέντε δραχμῶν  
**Translation:** *I buy for five drachmas*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (πέντε δραχμῶν) indicates the price paid.

---

### 12. 🏷️ Genitive of Measure/Extent
**Greek:** ἔμεινεν τριῶν ἡμερῶν  
**Translation:** *He stayed for three days*  
**Explanation:** Genitive (τριῶν ἡμερῶν) expresses the extent of time.

---

### 13. 🏷️ Genitive of Comparison
**Greek:** σοφώτερος τοῦ Σωκράτους  
**Translation:** *Wiser than Socrates*  
**Explanation:** Comparative adjectives often use the genitive for comparison.

---

### 14. 🏷️ Genitive Absolute
**Greek:** τοῦ ἀνδρὸς ἐλθόντος, ἐχάρησαν  
**Translation:** *When the man came, they rejoiced*  
**Explanation:** A genitive noun and participle form an adverbial clause independent of the main syntax.

---

### 15. 🏷️ Genitive of Respect (Specification)
**Greek:** δεινὸς τῇ μάχῃ  
**Translation:** *Formidable in battle*  
**Explanation:** The genitive (τῇ μάχῃ) specifies the respect in which someone is formidable.
