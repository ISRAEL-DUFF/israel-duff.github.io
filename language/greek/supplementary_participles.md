# 📚 Supplementary Participles in Attic Greek

---

## 📌 What is a Supplementary Participle?

A **supplementary participle** (συμπληρωματική μετοχή) is a **participle that completes the meaning of a verb**, much like a complementary infinitive in English (e.g., *"He began **to speak**"*). It is not circumstantial or adjectival—it is **required** by the verb, hence "supplementary."

---

## 🔧 Syntax of Supplementary Participles

### 1. **Agreement**
The participle agrees with the **subject or object** (depending on the construction) in:
- **Case**
- **Number**
- **Gender**

### 2. **Case Usage**
- **Nominative** if the participle agrees with the **subject** of the main verb.
- **Accusative** if the participle agrees with the **object** of the main verb (often in indirect statement constructions).
- **Genitive/Dative** in some idiomatic expressions or after impersonal verbs.

---

## 🔑 Verbs That Commonly Take Supplementary Participles

These verbs **require** a participle to complete their meaning:

### 🔹 Verbs of Perception
- **ὁρᾶν** – to see  
- **ἀκούειν** – to hear  
- **αἰσθάνεσθαι** – to perceive  

📝 Syntax:
- Subject of participle is usually **accusative** (if different from main verb’s subject).  
- The participle expresses the action perceived.

📍Example:
> **ὁρῶ τὸν ἄνδρα τρέχοντα.**  
> I see the man running.  
> `ὁρῶ` (I see) + `τὸν ἄνδρα` (object) + `τρέχοντα` (accusative participle)

---

### 🔹 Verbs of Knowing, Learning, Showing, Revealing
- **μανθάνειν** – to learn  
- **γιγνώσκειν** – to know  
- **διδάσκειν** – to teach  
- **σημαίνειν** – to signal/show

📍Example:
> **ἔμαθον αὐτὸν λελύκοτα.**  
> I learned that he had loosed (something).  
> `ἔμαθον` (I learned) + `αὐτὸν` (object) + `λελύκοτα` (perf. participle)

---

### 🔹 Verbs of Beginning, Continuing, Ceasing
- **ἄρχομαι** – I begin  
- **παύομαι** – I stop  
- **διατελῶ** – I continue

📍Example:
> **ἄρχεται γράφων.**  
> He begins writing.  
> `ἄρχεται` (he begins) + `γράφων` (present active participle)

---

### 🔹 Verbs of Mental Activity or Emotion
- **οἶδα** – I know  
- **ἐλπίζω** – I hope  
- **χαίρω** – I rejoice  
- **θαυμάζω** – I marvel  
- **ἀγνοῶ** – I do not know

📍Example:
> **οὐκ ᾔδειν αὐτὸν ἐλθόντα.**  
> I did not know that he had come.

---

## 🧠 Meaning and Aspect of the Supplementary Participle

| Tense of Participle | Aspect                     | Example                  |
|---------------------|----------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Present**         | Ongoing action             | γράφων – writing         |
| **Aorist**          | Completed action (simple)  | γράψας – having written  |
| **Perfect**         | Resulting state            | γεγραφώς – having written (with result) |

📝 The tense of the participle shows the **aspect**, not the time, relative to the main verb.

---

## 🧩 Supplementary Participle vs Infinitive

Some verbs can take **either** a supplementary **participle** or an **infinitive**, but the participle is often more vivid or specific, especially with **perception**.

| Verb       | With infinitive       | With participle                |
|------------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| ἀκούω      | ἀκούω αὐτὸν λέγειν      | ἀκούω αὐτὸν λέγοντα            |
|            | I hear him speak       | I hear him speaking           |

---

## 🔄 Typical Patterns

### 1. **Subject + verb + participle**  
> **ἐπαύσατο λέγων.**  
> He stopped speaking.

### 2. **Verb + object + participle (object = subject of participle)**  
> **ἔγνω αὐτὸν πεπραχέναι τοῦτο.**  
> He knew that he had done this.

### 3. **Impersonal + participle**  
> **δοκεῖ σοφὸς εἶναι.**  
> He seems to be wise.  
> → Here **δοκεῖ** is impersonal and takes a participle or infinitive.

---

## ✨ Special Notes

- Supplementary participles are **not adverbial** (unlike circumstantial ones).
- They are always **required by the main verb** for full meaning.
- Mastery of these is crucial for reading Plato, Thucydides, and other high-level Attic prose.

---

## 🧠 Advanced Complexities of Supplementary Participles
supplementary participles in Attic Greek can indeed become more complex, both syntactically and semantically, especially in higher-level prose like Thucydides, Plato, and Xenophon.

Here’s a deeper look into the complexities that can arise:

---

### 1. **Embedded Participial Clauses**
A participle can govern its own **complements and modifiers**, forming a **mini-clause** inside the sentence.

📍Example:
> **ᾔδει τὸν ἄνδρα τὴν μάχην νικήσαντα σοφίᾳ.**  
> He knew that the man had won the battle by skill.  

Breakdown:
- Main verb: **ᾔδει** (he knew)
- Object: **τὸν ἄνδρα**
- Supplementary participle: **νικήσαντα** (aorist)
- Participial clause: includes **τὴν μάχην** (object) + **σοφίᾳ** (instrumental dative)

This is more than just "he knew that X won" — the participle comes with its own syntactic satellite.

---

### 2. **Multiple Participles (Asyndeton or Syndeton)**
A verb can take multiple supplementary participles, either linked with καί or just juxtaposed (asyndeton).  

📍Example (asyndeton):
> **ἤκουσα αὐτὸν ἐλθόντα λαβόντα τὴν πόλιν.**  
> I heard that he came and took the city.

Here:
- **ἐλθόντα** and **λαβόντα** are both accusative aorist participles
- Both are dependent on **ἤκουσα**
- Coordinated without a conjunction (asyndeton)

📍Example (with syndeton):
>**ᾔδει αὐτὸν γράφοντα καὶ λέγοντα.**  
>He knew that he was writing and speaking.
---

### 3. **Infinitive + Participle Hybrid Constructions**
Some verbs allow a mixed construction: an infinitive for one part, and a participle for another.

📍Example:
> **οἶδα αὐτὸν λέγειν ἀληθῆ εἰπών.**  
> I know that he speaks the truth, having said it.

- Main verb: **οἶδα**
- Infinitive: λέγειν
- Supplementary participle: **εἰπών** explaining background/context of **λέγειν**
---

### 4. **Impersonal Verbs + Participles in Subtle Agreement**
Impersonal verbs like **δοκεῖ, φαίνεται, λέγεται**, often use **nominative** participles that agree with the **logical subject** (though it's not overtly expressed).

📍Example:
> **δοκεῖ σοφὸς ὤν γράφειν καλῶς.**  
> He seems to be wise and to write well.

- **σοφὸς ὤν** = nominative supplementary participle
- Modifies the unspoken subject of **δοκεῖ**
- Can combine with an infinitive for full meaning
---

### 5. **Supplementary Participles + Genitive Absolute**
You may see **both** in the same sentence, especially in complex narrative or philosophical passages.

📍Example:
> **ὁ στρατηγὸς ἔφη τοὺς πολεμίους φεύγειν, τῆς μάχης γενομένης.**  
> The general said that the enemies were fleeing, after the battle had taken place.

- Main verb: **ἔφη** (he said)
- Supplementary participle clause: **τοὺς πολεμίους φεύγειν**
- Circumstantial participle: **τῆς μάχης γενομένης** (genitive absolute)
---

### 6. **Participles as Object Complement or Appositive**
Sometimes a participle is used not directly with a verb, but as an **apposition to the object**, giving additional description that is functionally supplementary.

📍Example:
> **οὐκ ᾔδει τὸν φίλον, τραυματισθέντα.**  
> He did not recognize his friend, (when) he had been wounded.

- The participle τραυματισθέντα is appositional but still adds essential information.

---

### 7. **Voice Interactions: Active + Middle/Passive Participles**
A verb may take **a participle in a different voice**, which alters the interpretation significantly.

📍Example:
> **ᾔσθετο αὐτὸν παυόμενον.**  
> He perceived that he was ceasing.

---

### 8. **Intervention of Other Words**
In complex prose, authors may split the verb and its participle across long stretches of text.

📍Thucydidean style:
> **κατενόει τοὺς μὲν πολεμίους, διαπεφευγότας διὰ τῶν ὀρῶν σποράδην ὥστε μηδένα συνιέναι, ἐκπλαγέντας ὡς ἀπροσδοκήτως ἥκοντας.**  
>*He realized that the enemy, scattered through the mountains so that no one could understand (their formation), had fled in panic because of their unexpected arrival.*  

Here:
- κατενόει governs several participial constructions
- All connected to his perception of the enemies' behavior
- Involves passive, middle, perfect, and circumstantial nuance

---
## 🧩 Summary of Complex Constructions

| Complexity Type                       | Description                                    |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Nested participial clauses**        | Participle has its own object, modifiers, etc. |
| **Multiple participles**              | Coordinated participles describing a sequence  |
| **Mixed infinitive + participle**     | Hybrid expression of thought/action            |
| **Apposition or object complement**   | Participle explains/clarifies noun             |
| **Genitive absolute + supplementary** | Two participial constructions coexisting       |
| **Voice layering**                    | Active verb, middle/passive participle         |
| **Discontinuous syntax**              | Word order separating verb and participle      |
| **Participial chains**                | Multiple participles creating deep syntax      |

12. **ἔμαθον αὐτὸν λελύκοτα τὴν πόλιν.**

13. **εἶδον τὸν ἄνδρα εἰσελθόντα.**

16. **οἶδα τὸν ἀδελφὸν ποιήσαντα τοῦτο.**

17. **ἐνόμισαν αὐτὸν βασιλέα γεγονέναι.**

18. **θαυμάζω σε τοῦτο ποιήσαντα.**  
> I am amazed that you did this.  
- **ποιήσαντα** (aorist participle) gives completed action  
---

19. **ἤλπιζε τὸν παῖδα ἐλευσόμενον.**  
> He hoped that the boy would come.  
- **ἐλευσόμενον** (future participle) for intended/future action  
---

20. **φησὶ τοὺς πολίτας μὴ ἀκούσαντας διαφθαρῆναι.**  
> He says that the citizens, not having listened, were ruined.  
- **μὴ ἀκούσαντας** (negative participle) expressing cause with aorist passive main verb
