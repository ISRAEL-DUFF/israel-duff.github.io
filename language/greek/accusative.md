# 📚 The Accusative Case in Attic Greek

---

## 🔧 I. Core Syntactic Role

### 1. **Direct Object of a Transitive Verb**
- The accusative **typically marks the direct object**, i.e., the **entity directly affected** by the verb.

#### Examples:
- **Παῖδα βλέπω.**  
  *I see a child.*  
  → *παῖδα* = direct object of *βλέπω*.

- **Ἐπαίδευσε τοὺς νεανίας.**  
  *He educated the young men.*  
  → *τοὺς νεανίας* = object of *ἐπαίδευσε*.

---

## 🧱 II. Semantic Categories

Now let’s look at the semantic force of the accusative in different constructions:

---

### 📍 A. **Extent of Time or Space**
Accusative can denote **how long** or **how far**.

#### Examples:
- **Ἔμεινε τρεῖς ἡμέρας.**  
  *He stayed (for) three days.*  
  → accusative of **duration of time**.

- **Ἔδραμε δέκα στάδια.**  
  *He ran ten stades (units of distance).*  
  → accusative of **extent in space**.

---

### 📍 B. **Respect / Specification** ("Accusative of Respect")
Indicates **in what respect** something is true.

#### Examples:
- **Σῶς εἰσι τὸν λόγον.**  
  *They are sound in respect to reason.*  
  → *τὸν λόγον* = accusative of respect.

- **Καλὸς τὸ σῶμα.**  
  *Beautiful in body.*  
  → *τὸ σῶμα* specifies the respect in which someone is beautiful.

---

### 📍 C. **Subject of Infinitive** (Accusative + Infinitive Construction)
Used when a subject of an infinitive is different from the subject of the main verb.

#### Examples:
- **Οἶδα τὸν ἄνδρα ἀφικνεῖσθαι.**  
  *I know that the man is arriving.*  
  → *τὸν ἄνδρα* is subject of *ἀφικνεῖσθαι*, in accusative.

- **Λέγουσι τοὺς θεοὺς εἶναι ἀθανάτους.**  
  *They say the gods are immortal.*  
  → *τοὺς θεούς* = subject of *εἶναι*.

---

### 📍 D. **Internal Accusative** ("Cognate Accusative")
Where the object repeats the idea of the verb — it **reinforces the action**.

#### Examples:
- **Μάχην μάχεται.**  
  *He fights a fight.*

- **Ὄναρ ὄναρ βλέπει.**  
  *He sees a dream in a dream.*

---

### 📍 E. **Adverbial Accusative**
Used adverbially to express **manner, degree, cause, or time**.

#### Examples:
- **Θερμὸν ἔβρασεν.**  
  *He boiled it hot.* (manner)

- **Θάνατον κινδυνεύει.**  
  *He risks death.* (degree)

- **Ἕωθεν ἀνίσταται.**  
  *He rises early in the morning.* (“heōthen” is an adverbial form built on the accusative)

---

### 📍 F. **Double Accusative**
Some verbs take **two accusatives**: one of **person**, one of **thing**.

#### Categories:
1. **Verbs of teaching, asking, clothing, depriving**.

#### Examples:
- **Διδάσκω σε τὴν τέχνην.**  
  *I teach you the art.*

- **Αἰτοῦσι τὸν θεὸν χάριν.**  
  *They ask the god for a favor.*

- **Ἐστέρησεν αὐτοὺς τοῦ πλούτου.**  
  *He deprived them of wealth.* (here wealth is in genitive, but often takes accusative in other contexts)

---

### 📍 G. **Exclamatory Accusative**
Used in **exclamations**, often without a verb.

#### Examples:
- **Ὦ τὰν τύχην!**  
  *Oh the fortune!*

- **Ὦ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους!**  
  *Oh those men!*

---

### 📍 H. **Impersonal Expressions**
Used in **impersonal expressions** like *δεῖ, χρή*, with infinitive + accusative.

#### Examples:
- **Χρὴ σὲ φυλάττεσθαι.**  
  *It is necessary for you to be on guard.*

- **Δεῖ με ἀπιέναι.**  
  *I must go.*

---

# 🧬 Advanced and Complex Uses of the Accusative in Attic Greek

---

## 1. 🔁 **Accusative in Non-Finite Complement Clauses** (Nested ACI Structures)

### Example:
**Οἶδα τὸν φίλον φάσκειν τὸν ἄλλον εἶναι σοφώτερον.**  
*I know that my friend says that the other is wiser.*

---

## 2. ⚖️ **Double Accusative of Result and Respect**

### Example:
**Ἔλεγον αὐτὸν σοφὸν εἶναι.**  
*They said him (to be) wise.*

**Ἔδειξεν αὐτοὺς ἄξιους τοῦ στεφάνου.**  
*He showed them worthy of the crown.*

---

## 3. 🧱 **Accusative in Absolute-like Structures**

### Example:
**Τὸν ἥρωα κεῖνον πεσόντα εἶδον.**  
*I saw that hero having fallen.*

---

## 4. 🧙‍♂️ **Accusative of Cognate Result + Adverbial Accusative**

### Example:
**Τὴν μάχην ἐμάχοντο σφοδρὰν πολλὰς ἡμέρας.**  
*They fought the battle fiercely for many days.*

---

## 5. 🔄 **Reflexive/Reciprocal Accusative**

### Examples:
**Ἐτύπτετο τὴν κεφαλήν.**  
*He was beating his (own) head.*

**Ἔπαιον ἀλλήλους.**  
*They were hitting each other.*

---

## 6. 🎯 **Predicative Accusative in Equative Constructions**

### Example:
**Τὸν ἄνδρα σοφὸν ἡγοῦμαι.**  
*I consider the man wise.*

---

## 7. 📚 **Accusative in Narrative Elevation (Historical Infinitive)**

### Example:
**Καὶ τοὺς βαρβάρους φεύγειν.**  
*And (he says) the barbarians (began) to flee.*

---

## 8. 🧱 **Accusative in Enumerations or Appositions**

### Example:
**Τὸν Περικλέα, ῥήτορα, στρατηγὸν, καὶ ἄνδρα σοφόν, ἐτίμων.**  
*They honored Pericles — an orator, a general, and a wise man.*

---

## 9. 🏛️ **Idiomatic Prepositional Accusatives**

### Examples:
**εἰς ἔλεον πίπτειν** – *to fall into pity* → *to evoke pity*  
**κατὰ τὸν νόμον** – *according to the law*

---

## 10. 🌀 **Accusative of Effect with Middle Verbs**

### Example:
**Ἀχθομαι τὸν ἔλεον.**  
*I am burdened with pity.*

---

# 🧠 Wrap-Up: So Can It Get More Complex?

Yes. The accusative case in Attic Greek **goes far beyond "object of a verb."** It serves:

- as **subject of infinitives**  
- for **duration, distance, respect**  
- in **multiple layered constructions** with nested infinitives  
- in **predicative roles**  
- and in **stylistic or rhetorical uses** that evoke emphasis, emotion, or vivid narrative.


# 📦 Accusative Case in Attic Greek: 10 Basic + 10 Advanced Examples

---

## ✅ 10 Basic Examples of the Accusative

---

### 1. **Παῖδα βλέπω.**  
**"I see a child."**

- **Παῖδα**: accusative singular masculine of *παῖς*  
- **βλέπω**: present active indicative 1st person singular

👉 *Direct object* of the verb βλέπω.

---

### 2. **Τὸν λόγον γράφει.**  
**"He writes the speech."**

- **Τὸν λόγον**: accusative singular masculine of *λόγος*  
- **γράφει**: present active indicative 3rd person singular

👉 *Direct object* of γράφει.

---

### 3. **Ἔμεινε τρεῖς ἡμέρας.**  
**"He stayed (for) three days."**

- **τρεῖς ἡμέρας**: accusative plural feminine of *ἡμέρα*

👉 *Extent of time* — how long the action occurred.

---

### 4. **Ἔδραμε πέντε στάδια.**  
**"He ran five stades."**

- **πέντε στάδια**: accusative plural neuter of *στάδιον*

👉 *Extent of space* — how far he ran.

---

### 5. **Σῶς εἰσι τὸν λόγον.**  
**"They are sound in respect to reason."**

- **τὸν λόγον**: accusative singular masculine of *λόγος*

👉 *Accusative of respect* — specifies the domain.

---

### 6. **Οἶδα τὸν ἄνδρα ἀφικνεῖσθαι.**  
**"I know that the man is arriving."**

- **τὸν ἄνδρα**: accusative singular masculine of *ἀνήρ*  
- **ἀφικνεῖσθαι**: present middle infinitive

👉 Subject of the **infinitive** in an ACI construction.

---

### 7. **Μάχην μάχεται.**  
**"He fights a battle."**

- **Μάχην**: accusative singular feminine of *μάχη*  
- **μάχεται**: present middle/passive indicative 3rd person singular

👉 *Cognate accusative* — repeats verbal idea.

---

### 8. **Διδάσκω σε τὴν τέχνην.**  
**"I teach you the art."**

- **σε**: accusative singular of *σύ*  
- **τὴν τέχνην**: accusative singular feminine of *τέχνη*

👉 *Double accusative* — person and thing.

---

### 9. **Ὦ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους!**  
**"Oh the people!"**

- **τοὺς ἀνθρώπους**: accusative plural masculine of *ἄνθρωπος*

👉 *Exclamatory accusative* — emotional emphasis.

---

### 10. **Χρὴ σὲ φυλάττεσθαι.**  
**"It is necessary for you to guard yourself."**

- **σὲ**: accusative singular of *σύ*  
- **φυλάττεσθαι**: present middle infinitive

👉 *Subject of impersonal infinitive expression*.

---

## 🧠 10 Advanced Examples of the Accusative

---

### 1. **Λέγουσι τὸν ἄνδρα σοφὸν εἶναι.**  
**"They say the man is wise."**

- **τὸν ἄνδρα**: subject of infinitive  
- **σοφὸν**: predicate accusative

👉 *Subject + predicate in ACI*. This is a double accusative of **person and quality**.

---

### 2. **Ἔδειξεν αὐτὸν ἄξιον τοῦ στεφάνου.**  
**"He showed him worthy of the crown."**

- **αὐτόν**: accusative singular of *αὐτός*  
- **ἄξιον**: accusative singular masculine of *ἄξιος*

👉 *Double accusative*: person and predicate adjective (effect/result).

---

### 3. **Οἶδα τὸν φίλον λέγοντα τὸν ἄλλον εἶναι σοφώτερον.**  
**"I know that my friend says that the other is wiser."**

- **τὸν φίλον**: subject of *λέγοντα*  
- **τὸν ἄλλον**: subject of *εἶναι*

👉 *Nested accusative + participle + infinitive structure*.

---

### 4. **Ἐτύπτετο τὴν κεφαλήν.**  
**"He was beating his head."**

- **τὴν κεφαλήν**: accusative singular feminine of *κεφαλή*

👉 *Reflexive accusative* — emotionally emphatic.

---

### 5. **Ἔπαιον ἀλλήλους.**  
**"They were striking one another."**

- **ἀλλήλους**: reciprocal accusative plural masculine

👉 *Reciprocal pronoun* used as object of mutual action.

---

### 6. **Καὶ τοὺς πολεμίους φεύγειν.**  
**"And (he reports) the enemies to flee."**

- **τοὺς πολεμίους**: accusative plural of *πολέμιος*  
- **φεύγειν**: present infinitive

👉 *Historical infinitive usage* with **accusative subject**.

---

### 7. **Τὸν ἥρωα κεῖνον πεσόντα εἶδον.**  
**"I saw that hero having fallen."**

- **πεσόντα**: aorist active participle acc. masc. sg. of *πίπτω*

👉 A kind of *accusative absolute* or circumstantial participle attached to object.

---

### 8. **Τὴν μάχην ἐμάχοντο σφοδρὰν πολλὰς ἡμέρας.**  
**"They fought the battle fiercely for many days."**

- **σφοδρὰν**: accusative of manner  
- **πολλὰς ἡμέρας**: accusative of duration

👉 Multiple accusatives: cognate, manner, and extent.

---

### 9. **Τὸν Περικλέα, ῥήτορα, στρατηγὸν, καὶ ἄνδρα σοφόν, ἐτίμων.**  
**"They honored Pericles — an orator, a general, and a wise man."**

- **ῥήτορα, στρατηγὸν, ἄνδρα σοφόν**: appositional accusatives

👉 *Enumerative apposition* — stylistic expansion of the object.

---

### 10. **Ἀχθομαι τὸν ἔλεον.**  
**"I am burdened with pity."**

- **τὸν ἔλεον**: accusative singular masculine of *ἔλεος*

👉 *Mental-affective accusative* — the feeling one is burdened by.
