# 📘 Deep Dive into the Dative Case in Attic Greek

The **Dative case** in Attic Greek is a multifunctional case primarily associated with:

- **Indirect object** roles
- **Means/instrumentality**
- **Location and time**
- **Accompaniment and association**
- **Possession (especially in predicative contexts)**
- Various **idiomatic constructions**

We'll structure our deep dive into two major parts:

---

## 🧠 I. Syntactic Functions of the Dative Case

These refer to the grammatical roles the dative can take within a sentence.

### 1. **Dative of the Indirect Object**
Used with verbs of giving, telling, showing, saying, etc., to indicate **to/for whom** something is done.

> **δίδωσιν τῷ παιδί βιβλίον**  
> *He gives the book to the child.*

### 2. **Dative with Intransitive Verbs (Dative of Interest)**
Certain intransitive verbs take a dative object instead of an accusative.

> **πείθομαι τῷ ἀνδρί**  
> *I obey the man.*

> **χρῶμαι τούτῳ**  
> *I use this.*

These verbs require the dative in their lexicon entry.

### 3. **Dative of Possessor (Predicative Possession)**
The possessor is placed in the dative and typically appears with **εἰμί**.

> **ἐμοί ἐστιν οἰκία**  
> *I have a house.* (Lit. *There is a house for me.*)

> **οὐκ ἔστιν αὐτῷ φίλος**  
> *He has no friend.*

### 4. **Dative with Adjectives**
Often found with adjectives of **likeness, dissimilarity, friendliness, nearness**, etc.

> **φίλος τοῖς θεοῖς**  
> *Dear to the gods*

> **ἀλλότριος ἐμοί**  
> *Foreign to me*

---

## 🌐 II. Semantic Functions of the Dative Case

These focus on the *meaning* or *nuance* the dative adds to the clause.

### 1. **Dative of Means/Instrument**
Indicates the **instrument or means** by which an action is performed.

> **τοξεύει τῷ τόξῳ**  
> *He shoots with a bow.*

> **γράφει καλάμῳ**  
> *She writes with a reed.*

No preposition is used (unlike English); the dative alone suffices.

### 2. **Dative of Manner**
Describes **how** something is done. Often with an adjective.

> **λέγει παρρησίᾳ**  
> *He speaks with boldness.*

> **μάχεται θάρρει**  
> *He fights with courage.*

### 3. **Dative of Cause**
Shows the **reason or cause** of an emotion or action.

> **χαίρει τῇ νίκῃ**  
> *He rejoices because of the victory.*

> **ἀχθεῖται τῇ τύχῃ**  
> *He is annoyed at his fortune.*

### 4. **Dative of Time (When)**
Specifies **when** something happens—usually **within a general time frame**.

> **τῇ τρίτῃ ἡμέρᾳ**  
> *On the third day*

> **τῷ θέρει**  
> *In the summer*

### 5. **Dative of Place (Where)**
Very rare without prepositions, but in **poetic or old prose**, it can indicate **location**.

> **ἕστηκε πυλῇ**  
> *He stands at the gate.*

(More commonly, **ἐν + dative** is used.)

### 6. **Dative of Respect/Specification**
Specifies **in what respect** something is true.

> **τυφλὸς τοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς**  
> *Blind in the eyes* (i.e., physically blind)

> **σοφὸς τῇ γνώμῃ**  
> *Wise in judgment*

### 7. **Dative of Advantage/Disadvantage (Dativus Commodi/Incommodi)**
Indicates **for whom** (benefit or harm) the action is done.

> **πέμπει τοῖς φίλοις βοήθειαν**  
> *He sends help for his friends.*

> **συντάττει τῷ στρατηγῷ παγίδα**  
> *He arranges a trap against the general.*

### 8. **Dative of Accompaniment**
Used poetically or in formulaic phrases, though usually requires **μετά + genitive** in prose.

> **ἦλθε τῷ φίλῳ**  
> *He came with his friend.*

(More common prose form: **μετὰ τοῦ φίλου**)

### 9. **Dative with Compound Verbs (Prefix + Verb)**
Many compound verbs take a dative because the prefix governs it.

> **ἐπιτρέπει τῷ στρατηγῷ τὴν πόλιν**  
> *He entrusts the city to the general.*

> **προσχωρεῖ τοῖς πολεμίοις**  
> *He surrenders to the enemies.*

---

## 🏛️ Special Constructions and Idioms

### 1. **Double Dative**
A construction where two datives occur: **dative of purpose** + **dative of person**.

> **παρακαταθήκην αὐτῷ ἔθηκε**  
> *He gave it to him as a deposit.*

> **παραδείγματι τοῖς ἄλλοις ἔθηκε**  
> *He set him as an example for the others.*

### 2. **Dative Absolute**
Rare. Unlike the genitive absolute, the dative absolute is found occasionally with **impersonal expressions** and participles of verbs like **δοκεῖ**.

> **δοκοῦντι τοῖς Ἀθηναίοις**  
> *As it seemed good to the Athenians.*

## 🧠 Advanced & Complex Uses of the Dative in Attic Greek

---

### 1. 🧬 **Dative of Ethical Interest (Ethical Dative)**

> **τί μοι καὶ σοὶ, ὦ φίλε;**  
> *What [is it] to me and you, dear friend?*

> **ἀπέθανεν μοι ὁ παῖς.**  
> *My son died on me.*

---

### 2. 🪤 **Dative in Irony, Indignation, or Rhetorical Address**

> **οὐ σοι δοκεῖ;**  
> *Oh, doesn't it seem so to you?*

> **λέγε δή μοι, ὦ Σώκρατες.**  
> *Come now, tell me, Socrates (for *my* sake).*

---

### 3. 🔁 **Dative of Reference in Periphrastic Expressions**

> **ἡμῖν ἐστιν μαθεῖν.**  
> *It is ours to learn* → *We must learn.*

> **σοι ἐστι ποιητέον.**  
> *It must be done by you.*

---

### 4. 🔧 **Dative of Result or Condition**

> **ἀρκέσει τῇ φύσει.**  
> *It will suffice for nature.*

> **κακὸν τοῖς βουλομένοις μανθάνειν.**  
> *A bad thing for those wishing to learn.*

---

### 5. 🧩 **Dative with Verbal Adjectives and Participles**

> **ἔστι μοι ποιητέον.**  
> *I must do it.*

> **οὐκ ἦν σοι φευκτέον.**  
> *You should not have fled.*

---

### 6. 🧠 **Dative of Dynamic Equivalence (Functional Complementarity)**

> **ἐνέτυχον τῷ βασιλεῖ.**  
> *I encountered the king.* / *I appealed to the king.*

---

### 7. 🏗️ **Dative in Anacoluthon or Hyperbaton**

> **τῷ δὲ δή, ὅτι ἡγεῖται τῶν φίλων…**  
> *To him then — since he is considered a leader among friends...*

---

### 8. 🧠 **Rhetorical Dative / Appeals to the Audience**

> **σφίσι δέ, ὦ ἄνδρες Ἀθηναῖοι, τί χρὴ ποιεῖν;**  
> *What, O men of Athens, should be done for them?*

---

### 9. 🎭 **Poetic or Archaic Dative of Direction (Without Preposition)**

> **βῆ δὲ Διὶ μῆτιν.**  
> *He went to Zeus in counsel.*

> **ἦλθε τῷ οἴκῳ.**  
> *He came home.*

---

### 10. ⚖️ **Substituting for a Genitive or Accusative**

> **αἱρετός ἐστιν ἡμῖν.**  
> *He is preferable to us.*

> **ἡδὺς ἐμοί.**  
> *Pleasant to me*

---

# 📘 20 Examples of the Dative Case in Attic Greek  
**With explanations for syntactic and semantic categories**

---

## 1. **Dative of Indirect Object**

> **δίδωσιν τῷ ἀνδρὶ τὸ βιβλίον.**  
> *He gives the book to the man.*

- The dative **τῷ ἀνδρί** shows **to whom** the book is given — the **indirect object**.

---

## 2. **Dative with Intransitive Verb**

> **πείθεται τῷ διδασκάλῳ.**  
> *He obeys the teacher.*

- Certain verbs like **πείθομαι** require a dative; here, **τῷ διδασκάλῳ** is the object of obedience.

---

## 3. **Dative of Possession (Predicative)**

> **ἐμοί ἐστιν ὁ φίλος.**  
> *I have a friend.*

- The dative **ἐμοί** marks the **possessor** in a construction with **εἰμί**.

---

## 4. **Dative with Adjective**

> **ἀλλότριος ἐμοί ἐστιν.**  
> *He is alien to me.*

- The adjective **ἀλλότριος** governs the dative **ἐμοί**, indicating reference.

---

## 5. **Dative of Means/Instrument**

> **κόπτει τῷ ξίφει.**  
> *He strikes with the sword.*

- The dative **τῷ ξίφει** shows the **means** by which the action is performed.

---

## 6. **Dative of Manner**

> **λέγει παρρησίᾳ.**  
> *He speaks with boldness.*

- **παρρησίᾳ** (frankness) in the dative indicates **manner**.

---

## 7. **Dative of Cause**

> **χαίρει τῇ νίκῃ.**  
> *He rejoices at the victory.*

- **τῇ νίκῃ** shows **cause** — *because of* the victory.

---

## 8. **Dative of Time (When)**

> **τῇ τρίτῃ ἡμέρᾳ ἀφικνεῖται.**  
> *He arrives on the third day.*

- The dative **τῇ τρίτῃ ἡμέρᾳ** specifies **when** the action occurs.

---

## 9. **Dative of Place (Rare)**

> **ἕστηκε πυλῇ.**  
> *He stands at the gate.*

- Rare in prose; here, **πυλῇ** indicates **location**.

---

## 10. **Dative of Respect (Specification)**

> **σοφὸς τῇ γνώμῃ.**  
> *Wise in judgment.*

- The dative **τῇ γνώμῃ** specifies **in what respect** someone is wise.

---

## 11. **Dative of Advantage**

> **μαχόμεθα τῷ δήμῳ.**  
> *We fight for the people.*

- **τῷ δήμῳ** indicates the **beneficiary** of the action.

---

## 12. **Dative of Disadvantage**

> **παγίδα τῷ ἡγεμόνι ἔστησαν.**  
> *They set a trap against the leader.*

- The dative **τῷ ἡγεμόνι** implies **harm** or **disadvantage**.

---

## 13. **Dative of Accompaniment (Poetic)**

> **ἔρχεται τῷ ἑταίρῳ.**  
> *He comes with his companion.*

- Poetic or elevated use; prose prefers **μετά + gen.**

---

## 14. **Dative with Compound Verb**

> **προσχωρεῖ τοῖς πολεμίοις.**  
> *He surrenders to the enemies.*

- The compound verb **προσχωρέω** takes a dative **complement**.

---

## 15. **Double Dative**

> **παραδείγματι τοῖς πολίταις ἔθηκε.**  
> *He set him as an example for the citizens.*

- **παραδείγματι** = dative of **purpose**  
- **τοῖς πολίταις** = dative of **recipient**

---

## 16. **Dative Absolute (Rare, Impersonal)**

> **δοκοῦντι τοῖς Ἀθηναίοις, ἔπραξαν ταῦτα.**  
> *As it seemed good to the Athenians, they did this.*

- Impersonal verb + participle + dative = **dative absolute**.

---

## 17. **Ethical Dative**

> **τί σοι ταῦτα;**  
> *What are these things to you?* (implies emotional involvement)

- **σοι** expresses the **speaker's appeal** to the listener's concern.

---

## 18. **Dative of Necessity (Periphrastic Expression)**

> **ἐμοὶ ἐστὶ ποιητέον.**  
> *I must do [it].*

- **ἐμοί** is the **logical subject** of the necessity expression with verbal adjective.

---

## 19. **Dative of Condition / Sufficiency**

> **ἀρκεῖ μοι τὸ παρόν.**  
> *The present is enough for me.*

- **μοι** sets the **standard of sufficiency** — the condition under which it's enough.

---

## 20. **Poetic Dative of Direction**

> **ἦλθε τῷ οἴκῳ.**  
> *He came to the house.*

- Rarely, poetry uses dative instead of **εἰς + accusative** for **direction**.
