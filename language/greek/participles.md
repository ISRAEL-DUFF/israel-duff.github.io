# 📚 Attic Greek Participles  

## 1. What is a Participles?
Participles are verbal adjectives, and their syntax reflects this hybrid nature. They can behave **adjectivally, verbally**, or **adverbially**, depending on how they relate to the rest of the sentence.

A **participle** is a verbal adjective. It:
- Has **tense** and **voice** like a verb
- Has **case**, **number**, and **gender** like an adjective
- Can take **objects** and **adverbial modifiers** like a verb

---

## 🧩 2. Uses of Participles
Attic Greek participles fall into two main categories:

### ✅ Attributive
Describes a noun directly:
> **ὁ γράφων ἀνήρ** – *the man who is writing*  
>(literally, "the writing man")

### ✅ Supplementary
Used to **complete the meaning** of certain verbs that govern a participle. These verbs include:

| Verb     | Meaning         |
| -------- | --------------- |
| ἄρχομαι  | I begin         |
| παύομαι  | I cease         |
| φαίνομαι | I appear        |
| λανθάνω  | I escape notice |
| τυγχάνω  | I happen to     |
| φθάνω    | I anticipate    |

**Example:**

> **ἤρξατο γράφων.** – *He began writing.*  
- γράφων is the participle completing ἤρξατο
- The subject of both is the same (he)

More Info on [Supplementary Participles](supplementary_participles.md)

### ✅ Circumstantial (Adverbial)
Gives background information like:
- **Time**: when
- **Cause**: because
- **Manner**: how
- **Concession**: although
- **condition**: if

> **γράφων ταῦτα, ἔκλαυσεν.** – *While writing these things, he wept.*


| Kind        | Question Answered  | Common Translation   |
| ----------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| Temporal    | When?              | *while, when, after* |
| Causal      | Why?               | *because*            |
| Concessive  | In spite of what?  | *although*           |
| Conditional | On what condition? | *if*                 |
| Purpose     | For what purpose?  | *in order to* (rare) |
| Manner      | How?               | *by doing X*         |

- More Info on [Circumstantial Participles](attic-circumstantial-participles.md)
---

### ✅ Genitive Absolute
- Formed with a **noun/pronoun + participle** in the **genitive**
- Subject is **different** from that of the main clause
- Usually **circumstantial**, like adverbial participles  

Example:

>**τοῦ ἡλίου ἀνατέλλοντος, ἐπορεύθημεν.**  
>*While the sun was rising, we set out.*

- More info can be found here   [Genitive Absolute](genitive-absolute.md)

## 🧠 3. Aspect and Tense (not time!)
In participles, **tense = aspect**, not exact time:

| Tense     | Aspect       | Meaning                       |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| Present   | Ongoing      | γράφων → *writing (in progress)*            |
| Aorist    | Simple       | γράψας → *having written*     |
| Perfect   | Completed    | γεγραμμένος → *having written (and still so)* |
| Future    | Purpose      | γράψων → *about to write (or going to write)*     |

## 🗣️ 4. Voice
Participles exist in all three voices:

| Tense     | Active       | Middle         | Passive           |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Present   | γράφων       | γραφόμενος     | — (same as middle)|
| Aorist    | γράψας       | γραψάμενος     | γραφείς           |
| Perfect   | γεγραφώς     | γεγραμμένος     | γεγραμμένος       |
| Future    | γράψων       | γράψομαι        | γραφήσομαι        |

(Note: Future passive is rare and mostly periphrastic)

---

## 📑 5. Declension
Participles decline just like adjectives:

- Must agree with the noun they describe in gender, number, and case
- Often use 3rd declension endings

Example: **γράφων** (masculine nom. sg.) declines:

| Case | Masc.      | Fem.        | Neut.     |
|------|------------|-------------|-----------|
| Nom  | γράφων     | γράφουσα     | γράφον     |
| Gen  | γράφοντος  | γραφούσης    | γράφοντος  |
| Dat  | γράφοντι   | γραφούσῃ     | γράφοντι   |
| Acc  | γράφοντα   | γράφουσαν    | γράφον     |

## 🛠️ Key Syntactic Observations

| Syntax Type       | Uses Article? | Modifies Noun? | Subject Match? |
| ----------------- | ------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| Attributive       | ✅ Yes         | ✅ Yes          | ✅ Yes          |
| Supplementary     | ❌ No          | ❌ No           | ✅ Yes          |
| Circumstantial    | ❌ No          | ❌ No           | ✅ Yes          |
| Genitive Absolute | ❌ No          | ❌ No           | ❌ No           |

---

## 🔎 Word Order Notes
Participles are **very flexible** in word order
- Often placed **before** the main verb
- If **attributive**, follows the usual rules (inside or outside the article-noun sandwich)
- May carry **important narrative or rhetorical weight** depending on placement

---

## 🧠 Interpreting a Participle in Context
To understand a participle's syntax:
1. **Find its noun** (does it match the subject?)
2. **Check for an article**
3. **See if it's completing a verb** (supplementary)
4. **See if it’s in genitive absolute** (different subject)

## ✍️ 6. Examples by Tense  
📌 **Present Active (Ongoing)**  
>**ὁ παῖς τρέχων** – *the boy running*

📌 **Aorist Active (Completed)**  
>**ὁ στρατιώτης γράψας ἐπέστρεψεν.**  
>*The soldier, having written, returned.*  

📌 **Perfect Middle/Passive (State/result)**  
>**ὁ λόγος γεγραμμένος ἐστιν.**
>*The speech is written.*

📌 **Future Active (Intended)**  
>**οἱ μέλλοντες μαχεῖσθαι** – *those about to fight*

## 7. Active vs Passive Participles
Understanding the difference between active and passive participles in Attic Greek hinges on who is doing the action versus who is receiving it—just like with active and passive verbs. But because participles function like adjectives, the interpretation also depends on what noun the participle is modifying.


### 🔁 Quick Summary
| Voice       | Meaning                          | Example Translation        |
| ----------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Active**  | The noun **does** the action     | *the man writing*          |
| **Passive** | The noun **receives** the action | *the letter being written* |

### 🧠 Key Idea:
>**Active participle** → subject **is performing** the action  
>**Passive participle** → subject **is undergoing** the action

| Voice    | Who does the action?         | Example                       |
|----------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Active   | Subject does the action      | ὁ γράφων – *the man writing* |
| Passive  | Subject receives the action  | ἡ γραφομένη – *the letter being written* |

### 📌 Examples
**1. Active Participle**
>**ὁ γράφων ἀνήρ**  
>The man who is writing  
>→ The man is doing the writing.

- **γράφων** = present active participle of **γράφω** ("I write")
- Describes an ongoing action that he is doing

**2. Passive Participle**  
>**ἡ ἐπιστολὴ γραφομένη**  
>The letter being written
>→ The letter is having something done to it.

- **γραφομένη** = present passive participle
- Describes an ongoing action that the letter is undergoing

**3. Aorist Comparison (Completed Action)**  
>**ὁ γράψας τὴν ἐπιστολήν** – *the one who wrote the letter*  
>*(active, he did the writing)*  

>**ἡ ἐπιστολὴ γραφεῖσα** – *the letter that was written*  
>*(passive, the letter received the action)*

## 8. Active/Passive Practice Examples

### 1. ὁ παῖς τρέχων
- Active → *The boy who is running*

### 2. ἡ θυρὰ κλειομένη
- Passive → *The door being closed*

### 3. οἱ ἄνδρες παύσαντες τὸν πόλεμον
- Active → *The men who ended the war*

### 4. τὸ δῶρον δοθέν
- Passive → *The gift that was given*

### 5. ἡ γυνὴ λελαληκυῖα
- Perfect active → *The woman who has spoken*

---

### 🔍 Tip: Look for the Agent
Ask: Is the noun doing the action (use active)?  
Or is it receiving the action (use passive)?
